## Key Idea

The metrics used in traditional network routing include parameters like bandwidth, delay, hop count, etc.; however, they never considered carbon emissions. This paper explores the potential of using carbon intensity and the router power consumption in making routing decisions to lower the carbon emission level. The researchers present CATE which is a carbon-aware traffic engineering algorithm and which reroutes traffic on routes with less carbon emission while turning off those links which are not being used much.

## Critique

The approach seems quite convincing in that the authors consider different routing parameters based on actual wide area networks (WANs), actual patterns of traffic, and real carbon intensity data. However, the experiments conducted by the authors rely on simulations and use the estimated amount of power consumption of routers that differ from those used in practice, which makes it hard to tell whether the obtained results could be applied in practice. Also, CATE does not optimize for QoS.

## Connections

This is similar to the ECMP paper in the sense that both deal with ways in which routing may be used to enhance the performance of networks, but CATE is concerned about reducing carbon emissions as opposed to balancing traffic. This is related to the Raha paper in the sense that both papers examine the behavior of WANs when there are variations in network topology.
