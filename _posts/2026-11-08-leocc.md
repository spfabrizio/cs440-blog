## Key Idea

Changes in LEO satellite networks in terms of the link capacity, delay, and packet loss happen frequently due to the moving satellites and connection reconfiguration. However, conventional congestion control algorithms tend to view such changes as an indication of network congestion, which eventually leads to either low throughput or high delay. To solve this problem, LeoCC congestion control algorithm was developed. This algorithm is able to detect connection reconfigurations and adjust the sending rate according to the network changes.

## Critique

The approach appears to be persuasive as the authors implement LeoCC in the Linux kernel and assess the performance of their algorithm in real Starlink networks as well as controlled experiments. It is shown that there is an increase in throughput in comparison with the traditional congestion control algorithms like Cubic, Copa, and BBRv3. However, the tests conducted are dedicated mainly to the Starlink, and only three satellite terminals are used. Thus, it is hard to estimate whether the similar outcomes can be expected for other LEO satellite networks.

## Connections

This is similar to the in-network congestion signals paper in that both discuss the shortcomings of traditional congestion signals, but while the former talks about the limitations of in-network congestion signals, LeoCC discusses the problem of packet loss and delays caused by satellite dynamics. This is also similar to the dcSim paper in the sense that both papers look at ways of improving network performance in difficult situations.
