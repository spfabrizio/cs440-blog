## Key Idea
The Tor Hidden Services enable websites to operate anonymously, yet the Hidden Service providers remain vulnerable to deanonymization attacks since they need to remain online to serve their clients. In this paper, the authors present an architecture named VaulTor, which enables the hosting of a Hidden Service by a volunteer node referred to as a vault on behalf of the Hidden Service provider. The application is executed within a Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) by the vault; hence, the vault owner cannot access or modify the hosted content.

## Critique
This approach seems plausible since the authors use Intel SGX for implementation of VaulTor and test their system on the actual Tor network. The results they obtain reveal that the added security features incur minimal performance overhead. However, the security assessment does not consider side-channel attacks against TEEs, and these may leak sensitive information. Moreover, they have used time to first byte (TTFB) and time to last byte (TTLB) as metrics for evaluation in their experimental setup, which makes it hard to conclude about application-level performance.

## Connections
This is similar to the OpenFLAME research work since the two discuss distributed architectures where the services do not depend on a particular provider but on external infrastructure. In contrast to OpenFLAME that deals with federated spatial naming, VaulTor deals with the protection of anonymity of Hidden Service providers. This also links to the Internet availability research work since VaulTor allows the hosted content to remain available even when the Hidden Service provider is offline.
