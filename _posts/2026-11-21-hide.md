## Key Idea
BGP hijack attacks enable attackers to divert Internet traffic via their own network infrastructure, compromising privacy while still delivering traffic to the intended destination. HiDe is proposed by the authors, which is based on passive round-trip time (RTT) measurements for detecting long-distance BGP interception attacks. The basic principle behind this is that redirection through geographically distant locations increases propagation delay, which can be detected through abrupt spikes in minimum RTT measurements. The HiDe system detects such spikes in round-trip times through changepoint detection at the IP-prefix level using programmable switches. The authors show that HiDe can detect an interception attack in 500 milliseconds.

## Critique
This approach looks plausible since the authors test their system on actual campus network traffic and ethically conducted BGP interception attacks. The experiment results show that passive RTT measurements are capable of detecting the attacks without introducing probing overhead during normal operation. Nevertheless, the detection effectiveness of the approach depends on the geographic distance of the traffic diversion. It is hard to detect attacks involving shorter detours or nearby countries. Besides, the real-world evaluation was based on only a few controlled interception attacks.

## Connections
This is related to the VaulTor article in that both are concerned with protecting the user from threats that can expose sensitive information over the network but, whereas the former addresses deanonymization attacks, the latter detects traffic interception using BGP hijacking. Additionally, this work relates to BISCAY in that both of them use network measurements in order to respond to network conditions, albeit BISCAY uses radio KPIs to do so.
