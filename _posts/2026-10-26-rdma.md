## Key Idea

RDMA networks commonly use PFC to avoid losing packets, but this poses issues like head-of-line blocking and poor scalability. The proposed solution is DCP, which is a transport architecture that leverages customized RNICs and switches to enhance reliability in lossy fabrics. When the data queue becomes congested, the switch trims packet payloads and forwards header-only (HO) packets. This makes it easier for senders to detect lost packets and retransmit them without relying on retransmission timeouts in normal operation.

## Critique

The rationale behind the approach is very compelling, as the authors implement DCP using P4 switches along with FPGA-based RNICs and evaluate the effectiveness of the approach using testbed experiments and simulations. Nevertheless, since DCP needs changes to the switches and RNICs, it is questionable whether this approach could be adopted in current datacenters. In addition, as DCP uses a nearly lossless control plane, it would be fascinating to see its performance when header-only packets are lost during congestion.

## Connections

This is similar to dcSim since both systems try to ensure reliability in lossy data center networks, although dcSim probes paths before sending packets, while DCP employs packet trimming in case of congestion. It is also relevant to the ECMP paper since both discuss traffic management techniques, although the ECMP paper focuses on flows while DCP supports packet-level load balancing.
