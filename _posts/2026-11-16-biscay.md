## Key Idea
Frequent variations in the available bandwidth in mobile networks because of wireless channel conditions, mobility, and handovers make it hard for existing congestion control algorithms to achieve high throughput and minimize delay. The authors propose a congestion control system called BISCAY, which employs radio KPIs from mobile devices' radio chipsets to determine the cellular link's available bandwidth. To implement its congestion control function, BISCAY utilizes a real-time radio KPI extraction tool called OpenDiag to monitor the radio KPIs and adjust the TCP congestion window accordingly.

## Critique
The approach appears promising as the authors implement BISCAY on Android 5G devices and test it under both real-world 4G/5G network conditions and trace-driven emulations. It is shown that there is a significant reduction in average and tail delays along with similar or better throughput compared to other approaches. Nevertheless, BISCAY depends on access to radio chipset diagnostic interfaces and needs a custom Android update.

## Connections
This applies to the LeoCC paper since both use congestion control to adapt to the changing wireless network environment, even though LeoCC concentrates on satellite reconfigurations while BISCAY utilizes radio KPI measurements. In addition, it is related to Vidaptive paper, which has similar objectives of improving video quality and reducing latency, but uses dummy packets to maintain network feedback whereas BISCAY uses the radio chipset to obtain cellular link bandwidth measurements.
